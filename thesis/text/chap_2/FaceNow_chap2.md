*Facial Recognition Algorithms*   
It is possible to create a face recognition system using various algorithms to identify people. There are two popular ways to do so: standard feature analysis algorithms and deep learning techniques.

*Local Binary Pattern Histogram (LBPH)*  
Local Binary Pattern Histogram (LBPH) is a traditional facial recognition algorithm that analyzes texture patterns in grayscale images. Sanjay and Deva Priya (2022) assert that LBPH analyzes the luminance of adjacent pixels to generate binary patterns that represent facial skin appearance. The patterns are subsequently converted into histograms that depict the faces. LBPH is widely used in facial recognition systems due to its user-friendliness, low processing resource requirements, and effectiveness with limited training data. Yudi and his team (2024) highlighted the usefulness of LBPH in resource-constrained environments, noting its low memory footprint and suitability for embedded systems and robotics. For instance, LBPH may continue to struggle with reliably recognizing individuals in complex scenarios where illumination, facial orientation, and other variables fluctuate.

*Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)*  
Deep learning algorithms, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) that use ArcFace and pre-trained models, are frequently employed for facial recognition. The researchers found that Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have the inherent capacity to learn hierarchical features from image data. Although a pre-trained model is used for feature extraction in this study, the algorithm independently extracts meaningful representations from the visual data through its specialized architecture. Robust and accurate face identification is achieved by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) when an angular margin is introduced via ArcFace. Advanced facial recognition systems often use deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) alongside metric learning techniques to create facial embeddings.   
The researchers found that adding an angular border to the ArcFace loss makes distinguishing facial characteristics much easier. ArcFace models generate clear facial embeddings, enabling accurate identity matching via similarity measures such as cosine distance.   
The current research employs a pre-trained ArcFace model with a ResNet50 backbone to generate 512-dimensional facial embeddings. It has been observed that similarity measures, such as cosine distance, are used to verify embeddings for identity recognition. Identification matching is tested using these specific similarity measures when analyzing embeddings.   
A comparative study of LBPH and CNN for an educational institution attendance monitoring system was conducted by Budiman et al. (2023). Previous studies reported that Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) outperformed Local Binary Pattern Histograms (LBPHs) in facial recognition tasks.   
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) require more computational power and larger datasets to train effectively. Accuracy in real-time monitoring reached high levels when facial recognition systems used Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), according to research by Long (2020). Open-source libraries, including OpenCV, Dlib, and TensorFlow, have enabled rapid development of facial recognition algorithms in Python-based systems (Nguyen-Tat et al., 2024; Budiman et al., 2023). Researchers have observed that these libraries play a crucial role in the development process.  
Frameworks like Django remain a popular choice for web development due to their scalability, security features, and suitability for machine learning applications (Solis, 2025). While Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) achieve high recognition performance, they require greater computational resources, which can pose challenges in resource-constrained environments (Yadav et al., 2022). The researchers agreed that resource management is a key factor here.

## **THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK**

The design of the facial recognition system for monitoring employee attendance in this study is based on several theoretical concepts in biometric systems. These ideas provide the basic understanding of how facial recognition technologies identify people and how algorithms analyze facial features to achieve accurate recognition.

*Biometric Authentication Theory*  
Biometric authentication theory explains how people are identified using unique biological traits. Unlike traditional methods that often use passwords or ID cards, biometric systems identify people based on unique physical or behavioral traits. Biometric identifiers include fingerprints, facial features, iris patterns, and voice recognition. Biometric authentication provides a reliable means of confirming employee identities before attendance is recorded within monitoring systems. Given that biometric characteristics are inherently unique to individuals, these systems help mitigate prevalent issues such as time theft, impersonation, and buddy punching.   
Facial recognition technology offers a contactless way to verify identity. Facial recognition systems identify people by analyzing unique facial features. This includes looking at the distances between key facial points, the surface textures, and the overall geometric patterns of the face.  
In this study, facial recognition is the primary biometric method used to track employee attendance. The design of the proposed system is supported by biometric authentication theory, which provides a reliable framework for verifying identity and attendance monitoring.

*Pattern Recognition Theory*  
Pattern recognition theory focuses on identifying patterns and regularities within datasets. In the domains of computer vision and image processing, pattern recognition methodologies are employed to analyze visual characteristics derived from images and subsequently categorize them into established classifications.  
Facial recognition systems are heavily dependent on pattern recognition principles, since each face exhibits distinctive patterns that result from facial structures and textural configurations. These patterns can be extracted from facial images and compared with stored patterns in a database to determine whether a match exists.  
The LBPH algorithm is a traditional pattern recognition method used in facial recognition systems. The method works by examining the local texture patterns in an image. These patterns are then converted to numerical values, allowing comparison with other images. As a result, the algorithm can identify unique facial features, helping to distinguish between different people.  
Pattern recognition theory provides the basis for understanding how facial features can be represented as recognizable patterns. This allows algorithms to classify these patterns, which is crucial for confirming identity

*Machine Learning Theory*  
Machine learning theory provides a glimpse into how computers extract meaning from the information they process. This process enables them to forecast outcomes or make choices, even when every possible scenario isn’t explicitly programmed. Machine learning models learn from large datasets to discover hidden patterns and relationships. These identified patterns then serve as the basis for tasks like classification and prediction. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), a type of deep learning architecture, are built on the principles of machine learning. These networks excel at tasks that require image recognition. Their ability to learn hierarchical features from visual data independently contributes to their effectiveness. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) use a series of processing steps to identify edges, textures, shapes, and complex patterns in images. This study utilizes a pre-trained model for feature extraction, thereby circumventing the need to train the CNN from the ground up, and subsequently converts these features into mathematical representations known as "feature embeddings." These embeddings are then cross-referenced with existing facial data to ascertain identity matches.  
In this study, the use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) is supported by machine learning theory. This theory explains how the algorithm can learn complex facial features and improve recognition accuracy by extracting features from data.

*Application of the Theoretical Framework in the Study*  
The theoretical concepts presented here form the basis for designing and implementing the FaceNow system, which uses facial recognition technology for attendance tracking. The theory behind biometric authentication explains how facial features can be used securely to verify identity. Furthermore, pattern recognition theory supports the application of the LBPH algorithm for analyzing facial features and identifying distinctive facial patterns.   
Machine learning theory provides the conceptual framework for convolutional neural networks, which can learn intricate facial representations from image data. Consequently, by synthesizing these theoretical foundations, this study establishes a structured methodology for evaluating the performance of LBPH and CNN algorithms in a real-time employee attendance monitoring system.
